# Facts in "หมูเล่าเรื่องเบคอน"

Retold in our own words from the topic of the short by KhunBomb (youtube.com/shorts/tRta-XfqdKE); its captions could not be fetched. Sources: standard accounts of Edward Bernays's 1920s campaign for the Beech-Nut Packing Company (Bernays's own recollections, e.g. his 1991 interview; widely retold, e.g. Wikipedia "Edward Bernays").

| On screen | Basis |
|---|---|
| In the 1920s many Americans ate a light breakfast (coffee, a roll or toast) | As described in accounts of the campaign. |
| Beech-Nut wanted to sell more bacon | Beech-Nut Packing Company hired Bernays to increase bacon sales. |
| Edward Bernays, "father of public relations" | Common epithet. |
| He did not advertise bacon directly; he asked doctors whether a hearty breakfast was better | Bernays had a physician write to about 5,000 doctors. |
| About 4,500 said yes | Bernays reported about 4,500 agreed. |
| Newspapers reported "doctors recommend a hearty breakfast" | The results were publicised in newspapers. |
| Bacon and eggs became the standard breakfast | The film frames this as the campaign's legacy; its exact effect on sales is not measured here. |
The pig narrator is a joke device; the Beech-Nut name appears as plain text, with no logo.
