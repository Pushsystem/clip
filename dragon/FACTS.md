# Facts in "ทำไมทั่วโลกมีมังกร"

Retold in our own words from the topic of "ทำไมทั่วโลกถึงมีตำนานมังกร" (Point of View, youtube.com/watch?v=kx3VKi8cK5U); its captions could not be fetched. The film presents explanations as theories.

| On screen | Basis |
|---|---|
| Europe: fire-breathing dragons; China: dragons linked to rain and water; Central America: a feathered serpent | Common features of these traditions (e.g. the Chinese dragon as a rain/water deity; the Mesoamerican feathered serpent, Quetzalcoatl / Kukulkan). Designs in the film are our own. |
| Theory 1: ancient people found giant bones they could not explain | Proposed by historians of science such as Adrienne Mayor (The First Fossil Hunters, 2000). |
| In China, fossils were ground and sold as medicine called "dragon bones" | "Longgu" (龙骨) in traditional Chinese medicine are fossil bones and teeth; the oracle bones were identified in 1899 among them. |
| Theory 2: an instinctive fear of predators — snakes, big cats, birds of prey — combined into one creature | Anthropologist David E. Jones, An Instinct for the Dragon (2000). |
| Theory 3: forces of nature people could not control (floods, storms, fire) | A widely cited interpretation, e.g. dragons as rain/river spirits in East Asia. |
| Dragons may be a fear the whole world shares | The film's conclusion, framed as "อาจ" (may). |
