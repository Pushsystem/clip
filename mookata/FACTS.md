# Facts in "หมูกระทะมาจากไหน"

Retold in our own words from the topic of the short "ต้นกำเนิด หมูกระทะ" (AUNZA THAILAND, youtube.com/shorts/A6C6vyT2g14); its captions could not be fetched. The origin of mookata is disputed, so the film presents the first three items as legends ("ตำนาน"), not established history.

| On screen | Status / basis |
|---|---|
| Mongol soldiers grilled meat on upturned iron helmets | A popular legend, often told for dome grills; no firm historical evidence. |
| In Japan the dome grill dish is called "Genghis Khan" (ジンギスカン), for almost 100 years | The Jingisukan mutton dish of Hokkaido is attested from the early 20th century (commonly dated to the 1920s–30s). |
| Older Thais called it "เนื้อย่างเกาหลี" (Korean grilled beef) | A commonly reported former name in Thailand; Korean ties to the dish itself are not established. |
| The Thai version adds a soup moat around the dome, fat drips into the soup, and a suki-style dipping sauce | Description of the dish as served in Thailand. |
| "The origin is still argued about" | Reflects that sources disagree. |
