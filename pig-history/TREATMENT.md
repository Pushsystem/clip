# หมูมาเอง (The Pig Came by Itself) — Treatment

Style: Mid-century Cartoon (`midcentury-toon`), chosen by the user after Comic History Cartoon could not get its image-generated puppets into this machine. Everything is drawn in code.
Format: vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, 40 s, for TikTok. Thai voice-over (edge-tts `th-TH-NiwatNeural`) and Thai captions only.
Source: the topic of the short "ทำไมมนุษย์โบราณ... ถึงรู้ว่าหมูกินได้?!" (อิหยังวะศาสตร์ EP.39), retold in our own words. Its captions could not be fetched; the facts are our own (FACTS.md). Nothing from that video is used.

## 1. Three structures

1. **A rules card**: five "rules of how to eat a pig", one medallion per rule, a held hunter figure. Clear, but rules are the wrong frame for history.
2. **Do / don't split**: "what people think" vs. "what really happened" in two colour blocks for each step. Good for the twist, but halves the picture on a phone.
3. **A classroom lesson in five numbered steps that ends on a reversal**: the question as the opening gesture, five medallions (try it → hunt it → settle down → the rubbish heap → generation after generation), then the punchline splits the frame and flips the question.

**Chosen: 3.** The style's grammar is the numbered "follow along" lesson; the history is a sequence, and the payoff (the pig domesticated itself) lands as the lesson's "result". The split frame from structure 2 is spent once, at the punchline.

## 2. Logline and arc

A Stone Age hunter wants to know who first knew pigs were edible. The lesson shows that nobody knew: people tried things, hunted boar for ages, then settled, and the boars came to the rubbish heap by themselves and turned into pigs.
- Setup (0–13 s): the question; step 1 trial and error.
- Turn (13–27 s): steps 2–5: hunt, village, rubbish heap, generations.
- Ending (27–40 s): China did the same; split-frame reversal; iris-out on the smug pig.

## 3. Benchmark

Learn from 1950s classroom shorts and limited-animation studios (one sentence per picture, a held figure whose hands do the work, colour-plane rooms, medallion step numbers) and from short-video history explainers (question in the first second, one joke per beat). Don't take their characters, drawings, lettering or music.

## 6. Cue map

Tempo 120 bpm (beat 0.5 s), bars of 2 s, F major. Cool-jazz combo: vibraphone, flute, pizzicato walking bass, brushes, finger snaps, celesta.
- 0–5.2: vibes arpeggio + brushes, bass walks in at 1.0.
- 5.2–13: each step gets its own vibraphone chord on its medallion (F6, Gm7, Am7, B♭maj7, C9); flute melody fragments; stamps on beats.
- 13–16.4: sticks on the ride, double-time feel, bongos.
- 16.4–23.6: back to brushes; celesta for huts; everything cuts 21.9–22.2 (silence) then the munch.
- 23.6–27.8: vibes glissando up per animal, starburst chord a step up (G).
- 27.8–30.8: celesta and bass only.
- 30.5–31.0: silence; 31–36: snaps + bass; ✗ a low vibes cluster, ✓ the full combo hit.
- 36.2–40: the five step chords return one by one, final F6/9 rings out.
